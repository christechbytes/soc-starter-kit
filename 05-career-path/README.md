# Career Path: From Career Changer to SOC Analyst

## My story

I spent 13 years as a chef before moving into cybersecurity. After living abroad for over a decade, I moved home and started over. I completed a cybersecurity certificate program, which led to an internship where I worked with a SIEM, cloud threat detection, firewalls, and compliance documentation.

If you're starting late or from a non-technical background, you're not behind. Your previous career gave you skills security teams need: communication, documentation, and judgment under pressure.

## My certification order

| Order | Certification | When | Why it helped |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | CompTIA Security+ | April 2025 | The foundation. Most entry level job posts ask for it |
| 2 | CompTIA Network+ | March 2026 | Made firewall and network work finally click |
| 3 | Microsoft Azure Fundamentals (AZ-900) | Sept 2026 | Cloud basics. I failed my first attempt and passed the second |

**Honest tip:** Many people take Network+ before Security+. Knowing how networks work makes security concepts easier to understand.

## How I study

- **Quiz first.** I take practice questions before reading, so I know what I don't know
- **Analogies.** I translate every new concept into an everyday comparison
- **Commute review.** Audio lessons and podcasts during travel time
- **Hands on beats videos.** Watching someone configure a firewall isn't the same as doing it

## What the job looks like day to day

- Reviewing security alerts and deciding which ones are real
- Following and writing runbooks (step by step guides) to investigate
- Writing documentation: change records, procedures, incident notes
- Asking colleagues for access or information you don't have
- Learning something new almost every day

**Reality check:** A lot of the job is writing and communication. If you're good at explaining things clearly, that's a real advantage.

## Free resources to build skills

| Resource | What it is |
|---|---|
| [Splunk Boss of the SOC (BOTS v3)](https://github.com/splunk/botsv3) | Free dataset from real style security incidents to practice investigating |
| [Atomic Red Team](https://github.com/redcanaryco/atomic-red-team) | Small tests that simulate attacker techniques, mapped to MITRE ATT&CK |
| [MISP](https://www.misp-project.org/) | Free, open source platform for sharing threat intelligence |

## What I'd tell my past self

1. Start before you feel ready
2. Build a home lab, even a small one with virtual machines
3. Document everything you learn. It becomes your portfolio
4. Ask questions. Nobody expects you to know everything
